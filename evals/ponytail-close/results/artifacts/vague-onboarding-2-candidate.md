# Customer Support Onboarding Guide

Your first-week goal is to handle routine customer requests and recognize when a case needs help. Send your first customer replies with your trainer’s review. Start handling routine cases independently once your manager confirms you’re ready.

## Day 1 · Get set up

Meet your manager and trainer. Before taking a ticket, confirm you can access:

- **Support inbox** for your assigned queue, conversation history, and ticket status.
- **Knowledge base and practice environment** for troubleshooting, approved procedures, and safe practice.
- **Team channel and escalation contacts** for questions during your shift and urgent issues.

Ask your trainer to show you where to find your queue’s response targets, identity verification procedure, approval rules, and escalation contacts. Flag missing access to your trainer before taking work that requires it.

## Days 2–3 · Shadow and practice

Watch your trainer handle a routine request and escalate a case. Then practice the same workflow in a training ticket.

1. **Read the full conversation.** Identify what the customer wants, what they’ve already tried, and any commitments a teammate made.
2. **Check the current procedure.** Verify the customer’s identity when required. Ask only for information needed to resolve the case. Never ask for a password or one-time verification code.
3. **Resolve the issue or set the next step.** Explain what you checked, what you did, and what happens next in plain language. If you need more time, give an update time you can meet.
4. **Record the outcome.** Leave a short internal note with the issue, checks performed, action taken, and any follow-up owner and deadline. Set the ticket status to match what happens next.

## Days 4–5 · Handle tickets with review

Start with common requests from your assigned queue. Have your trainer review each reply before you send it. For a handoff, include the ticket link, customer impact, what you’ve checked, and the decision or action you need. Confirm who owns the next customer update.

| If you encounter | Take this action |
|---|---|
| Suspected account takeover, exposed customer data, or a widespread outage | Use the urgent escalation route. Follow the incident procedure for customer updates. |
| A refund, account change, or exception outside your authority | Ask the designated approver before making the change or promising an outcome. |
| An unclear policy or a problem you can’t reproduce | Ask your trainer or the designated specialist. Tell the customer when they can expect an update. |

## Before handling tickets independently

Ask your trainer to review one routine ticket you handled from start to finish and one escalation you completed or practiced. You’re ready when you can find the applicable procedure, write a clear reply, record the outcome, and identify the owner and timing of any next step. Ask your manager to confirm which queue and request types you can handle independently.

*The first-week schedule is a suggested pace. Your trainer should confirm the team’s tools, response targets, approval limits, and escalation contacts on day one.*
