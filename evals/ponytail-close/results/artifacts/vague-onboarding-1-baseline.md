# Customer Support New Hire Guide

Your first-week goal is to handle a routine customer request from intake to resolution with a reviewer available. Your manager will confirm when you’re ready to take tickets independently and which queues you can work in.

## 1. Get ready to take tickets

On your first day, ask your manager to walk you through the following. Keep the links and contact names somewhere you can find them during a shift.

| Find or confirm | Check that you can |
|---|---|
| Support inbox and training queue | Open a ticket, find its history, and save an internal note |
| Knowledge base and policy library | Find current guidance for common requests, identity verification, and actions that require approval |
| Queue rules | Identify which tickets to pick up, how quickly to reply, and which status to use while waiting |
| Escalation contacts and incident channel | Reach the person covering your shift and find the backup contact |
| Reviewer and practice schedule | Share a draft for feedback and know who approves your first live replies |

Tell your manager about missing access before taking live work. Use the training queue or approved practice tickets while access is being set up.

## 2. Work a ticket from intake to resolution

1. **Read the full thread.** Identify what the customer wants, what they’ve already tried, and what the team has promised. Check for a related ticket or known incident before starting a new investigation.
2. **Verify before acting.** Follow the team’s identity verification process before sharing account details or making account changes. Use approved tools and ask only for the information needed to move the case forward. Never ask a customer to send a password or one-time code.
3. **Check the current guidance.** Use the relevant help article or policy. Ask your reviewer or shift lead if the policy is unclear, the action exceeds your authority, or the documented steps fail.
4. **Write the reply.** Start with the answer or current status. Give the customer a clear next step. If the issue is still open, say who will follow up and when, using a timeframe you can meet. Review the recipient and any attachments before sending.
5. **Record the outcome and set the status.** Note what you checked, what you changed, and what remains open. If the case is pending, record who owns the next action and when it’s due. Close the ticket when it meets the queue’s closure criteria.

**Example reply when you need more information**

> Hi Sam, I can help check why your export failed. Please send the error message and the approximate time it happened, including your time zone. You can leave out any customer data. Once I have those details, I’ll check the failed attempt and let you know the next step.

## 3. Escalate when the case needs another owner

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Possible account takeover, exposed customer data, or a widespread outage | Use the designated urgent escalation channel immediately and follow the incident procedure. |
| Refund, exception, or account change beyond your authority | Send the case to the authorized owner. Give the customer a status update while approval is pending. |
| Troubleshooting stalls or the case needs specialist access | Share the ticket link, customer impact, steps already tried, and the specific help you need. |
| A promised update is due before you have an answer | Send a progress update and record the next check-in. If your shift is ending, follow the handoff procedure and confirm who owns the follow-up. |

## 4. Complete your first-week check

Start by shadowing an experienced teammate. Then draft replies to practice tickets, review the feedback, and work live tickets with supervision once your manager approves.

Use one routine case and one escalation scenario for your readiness check. Ask your reviewer to confirm that you can find the correct policy, write a clear reply, protect customer information, and leave a record another teammate can pick up. Before taking tickets independently, confirm your approved queues and who to contact when you need help.

*Company-specific details to confirm on day one include tool names, policy links, response targets, approval limits, and escalation contacts.*
