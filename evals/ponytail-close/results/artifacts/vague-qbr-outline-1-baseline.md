# Quarterly business review deck outline

**Audience** — Customer executive sponsor, business owner, and product or operations lead  
**Purpose** — Agree on next quarter’s priorities and secure any decisions or commitments needed to deliver them.  
**Suggested format** — Six slides for a 30-minute meeting, with 10 minutes reserved for decisions and commitments.

| Slide | What to show |
|---|---|
| **1. This quarter’s result and next quarter’s recommendation** | Lead with the most important customer outcome and the recommended next priority. State the decision or commitment you need today, who can make it, and when it’s needed. |
| **2. Progress against agreed goals** | Show up to three customer goals in a scorecard with the baseline, quarter target, actual result, and variance. Use the customer’s success measures and label the reporting period and data source. If business impact is unmeasured, label any usage metric as a proxy. |
| **3. What drove the results** | Highlight the one or two workflows, teams, or milestones that explain the most important gains or gaps. Use a small trend chart or a concrete customer example. Distinguish observed changes from hypotheses about their cause. |
| **4. What needs attention** | Show only issues that could materially affect a customer goal. For each, state the impact, proposed action, owner, and due date. Flag any dependency that needs the sponsor’s help. |
| **5. Next quarter’s proposed plan** | Recommend up to three priorities. For each, show the intended customer outcome, success measure and target, first milestone, and accountable owner. If resources are limited, name the trade-off the customer needs to resolve. |
| **6. Decisions and commitments** | Record what was approved or deferred. Confirm the owner and due date for each next action, who will resolve each open decision, and the date of the next check-in. |

Write each slide title as a customer-specific takeaway once the evidence is available. For example, **“Onboarding time fell from 12 to 9 days, one day above target”** gives the sponsor the result and the gap at a glance. Keep detailed usage breakdowns and metric definitions in an appendix if they’re needed for follow-up.
