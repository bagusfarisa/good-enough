# Customer Support Onboarding Guide

In your first week, learn how to handle a support request from intake to resolution, practice on sample tickets, and have your team lead review your first live replies. Your lead will confirm when you can handle routine tickets independently.

## Before you take a live ticket

Confirm with your team lead that you can access the support inbox, internal knowledge base, and approved customer lookup tools. Find the current policies for identity verification, refunds and account changes, response targets, and escalation. Make sure you know who to contact if your lead is unavailable.

Review one resolved ticket and one escalated ticket with your trainer. For each, identify what the customer needed, what the agent checked, and who owned the next step.

## Handle a ticket from start to finish

1. **Read the full conversation.** Identify what the customer wants, what has already been tried, and whether there is an urgent issue. If another agent owns the ticket, coordinate with them before replying.
2. **Verify what you need.** Check the current policy before sharing account-specific information or making changes. Use the approved verification process when required. Never ask for a password or one-time code.
3. **Resolve what you can.** Use the knowledge base and approved tools. If you need more information, ask for the specific detail that will move the case forward. If the request is outside your authority, follow the escalation process.
4. **Reply in plain language.** Acknowledge the issue, explain what you did or what the customer should do next, and say when they can expect an update if the issue remains open. Give only timelines you can commit to.
5. **Record the outcome.** Add an internal note with the issue, relevant checks, and actions taken. Set the ticket status based on what happens next. Close it when it meets the team’s closure criteria. If it stays open, record who owns the next action and when it’s due.

## Escalate when you need help or approval

Use the designated urgent channel for suspected account compromise, privacy exposure, or a possible widespread outage. For requests outside your authority, such as a refund exception, route the ticket to the team or person who can approve it.

In the handoff, include the ticket link, customer impact, what you verified or tried, and the decision or action you need. Record who will update the customer and when. If ownership is unclear, ask your lead to assign it.

## Practice and get cleared for independent work

Draft replies for three sample cases with your trainer. Start with a routine question, then try a case that requires verification and one that requires escalation. When your lead assigns your first live tickets, get approval before sending each reply.

You’re ready for independent routine work when your lead confirms that you can find the applicable policy, handle customer information correctly, write a reply with a clear next step, and leave a ticket record another agent can pick up.

*This guide assumes a shared support inbox and a team lead who reviews early replies. Your team lead should confirm the tools, response targets, escalation contacts, and approval limits before you begin live work.*
