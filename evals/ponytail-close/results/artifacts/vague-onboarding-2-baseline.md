# Customer Support New Hire Guide

Your first goal is to take a routine customer request from first reply through resolution. Start by shadowing a teammate, then work through a few cases with your trainer. Before you handle the queue independently, have your trainer review a case you owned from start to finish.

## Before you take your first case

Ask your trainer to show you where to find:

- **Your queue and schedule.** Confirm which channels you cover, how to pick up a case, and which response targets apply.
- **Approved answers and account tools.** Find the knowledge base, saved replies, and tools you can use to check customer or account details.
- **Verification and privacy rules.** Learn when to verify a customer, what information you can share, and where to record sensitive details.
- **Escalation contacts.** Find the routes for urgent issues, suspected security or privacy incidents, and requests that require approval. Confirm who owns customer updates after a handoff.

## Work a case from first reply to resolution

1. **Read the history.** Identify what the customer wants to happen and what they or another teammate have already tried.
2. **Reply with a next step.** Acknowledge the specific issue. If you need more information, ask a focused question. If the request involves account access or sensitive information, follow the verification policy before sharing details or making changes.
3. **Investigate and act.** Use approved guidance and stay within your permissions. Record what you checked and what you changed so another teammate can pick up the case without repeating work.
4. **Escalate when needed.** Use the designated route for suspected security or privacy incidents, requests outside your authority, and issues that meet your team’s escalation criteria. Include the customer’s goal, the impact, what you checked, and what you need from the receiving team. Confirm who will send the next customer update.
5. **Give the customer a clear outcome.** Explain what was resolved and any step they need to take. If work is still pending, tell them what happens next and when they can expect an update you can commit to. Set the case status according to your queue’s rules.

**Example.** A customer says an export failed twice. Check the case history before asking them to retry. If the approved troubleshooting steps fail, send the receiving team the error, when it happened, and what you tested. Tell the customer when you’ll update them, even if the fix is still under investigation.

## Before you work independently

Review one case you handled with your trainer. Your customer reply should make the outcome or next step clear, and your internal notes should let another teammate continue the work. Practice a handoff if the case didn’t require one. Ask your trainer to confirm which case types you can handle on your own and where to get help with exceptions.
