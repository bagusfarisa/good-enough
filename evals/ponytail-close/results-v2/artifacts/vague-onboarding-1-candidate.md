# Customer support onboarding guide

Your first-week goal is to handle a routine customer request from intake to resolution under review. Your lead will confirm which queues and actions you can own independently. Until then, have your assigned reviewer approve customer replies and account changes before you send or apply them.

## Day 1 · Get access and meet your reviewer

Ask your lead to confirm:

- Who reviews your work and who covers when they’re unavailable.
- Which support queue to start in, which channels it serves, and which response targets apply.
- Where to find the help desk, knowledge base, team chat, and current policies for identity verification, refunds, and escalation.
- How to reach the on-duty lead and where to report urgent security, privacy, or service issues.

Sign in to the tools you need, open the assigned queue, and locate the escalation instructions. Tell your lead about any missing access before taking a ticket that depends on it.

## Days 2–3 · Shadow and practice

Walk through a routine request and an escalated request with your reviewer. For each, identify what the customer needed, which policy guided the response, who owned the next step, and what justified closing the ticket or handing it off.

Draft a reply and an internal note for a practice case. Ask your reviewer to check accuracy, tone, and whether another teammate could pick up the case from your note.

## Days 4–5 · Handle routine tickets under review

1. **Read the full thread.** Identify what the customer wants and check prior replies or related tickets. Use your queue’s urgency and response rules to decide what needs attention first.
2. **Verify and investigate.** Follow the approved identity checks before sharing account details or making account changes. Use the current knowledge base and record what you checked. If you need more information, ask for the minimum needed through an approved channel.
3. **Draft the reply.** State what you found, what the customer or support team should do next, and when you’ll update them if the issue is still open. Use a follow-up time you can meet. Get approval before sending while you’re under review.
4. **Escalate when needed.** Use the designated route for suspected security or privacy issues, possible outages, and requests outside your authority. In the handoff, include the ticket link, customer impact, what you checked, and what action or decision you need. Confirm who owns the next customer update. If ownership is unclear, contact the on-duty lead.
5. **Record the outcome.** Add an internal note with what happened and any next action, owner, and due time. Set the ticket status according to your queue’s rules. Close it when the resolution and any required customer follow-up are complete.

## First-week check-in

Review one routine ticket and one escalation example with your lead. You’re ready for the scope your lead approves when you can show that you followed policy, gave the customer a clear next step, and left a record another teammate could act on. Record the queues and actions you can own, who to ask for help, and any skill to practice next week.

*This guide assumes a ticket-based team and a first-week review. Your lead should confirm the tools, response targets, approval limits, and escalation contacts on day one.*
