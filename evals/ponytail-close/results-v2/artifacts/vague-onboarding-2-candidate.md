# Customer Support New Hire Guide

Your first milestone is to handle a routine customer request from start to finish with a teammate’s review. Start taking tickets independently once your manager confirms which queues and actions you’re cleared to handle.

## Get ready for your first ticket

On your first day, ask your manager to confirm your trainer, training queue, and where to find the current support policies. Then check that you can sign in to the ticketing system and knowledge base. Report missing access to your manager before working on live tickets.

With your trainer, locate the procedures for identity verification, refunds, account changes, and urgent escalations. Confirm who handles each escalation, how to reach them, and which response targets apply to your queue.

## Work a ticket from start to finish

1. **Read the full conversation.** Identify what the customer wants, what has already been tried, and whether anyone has made a promise. Ask for clarification if the answer would change your next step.
2. **Check the current policy.** Use the knowledge base to confirm the approved steps and what you’re authorized to do. Follow the required verification process before sharing account information or making changes. Never ask for passwords or one-time codes.
3. **Reply in plain language.** Acknowledge the issue and give the answer or next action. If you’re waiting on another team, tell the customer what happens next and when they can expect an update you can commit to.
4. **Record the outcome.** Note what you checked, what you did, and any follow-up you promised. Set the status and owner so the next person can pick up the ticket. Close it when it meets your queue’s closure criteria.

## Know when to hand off

- **Suspected account takeover, exposed customer data, or a possible widespread outage.** Use the designated urgent escalation route as soon as you identify the concern. Share the ticket link, observed symptoms, and known scope without copying sensitive data.
- **A request outside your authority or a policy you can’t confirm.** Pause the action and ask your trainer or the designated owner. Tell the customer what you’re checking and when you’ll update them.
- **An unresolved ticket at the end of your shift.** Follow your queue’s handoff process. Include the next action, who owns it, and when the customer is due an update.

## Check that you’re ready to work independently

Shadow a teammate, then draft a reply and ticket note for review. Next, work a routine ticket with your trainer available. You’re ready for manager sign-off when your trainer can confirm that you applied the right policy, gave the customer a clear next step, and left a ticket record another teammate could act on. Practice one escalation scenario so you can identify the right route and explain what to include.

*Before sharing with new hires, add your team’s tool links, queue names, response targets, escalation contacts, and closure criteria. Team-specific policies and access requirements were not provided.*
