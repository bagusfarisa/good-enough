# Customer Support Onboarding Guide

Your first goal is to handle a routine support ticket from start to finish. Start by shadowing a teammate and drafting replies for review. Send customer replies independently once your team lead approves the ticket types you can own. Until then, ask your assigned reviewer to approve each reply before you send it.

## 1. Get set up before handling tickets

Ask your team lead to confirm the following during your first shift.

| What you need | What to confirm |
|---|---|
| Support inbox and knowledge base | You can open an assigned ticket and find the current product and policy articles. |
| Queue and response targets | You know which tickets to pick up, how to identify urgent cases, and when customers are due an update. |
| Reviewer and escalation contacts | You know who reviews your replies, who covers when they’re away, and where to route urgent issues. |
| Account and policy permissions | You know which actions you can take, which need approval, and how to verify a customer’s identity before sharing account details or making changes. |

If access or instructions are missing, flag the gap to your team lead and use training cases or shadowing while it’s resolved.

## 2. Work a ticket from first read to close

1. **Understand the request.** Read the full thread and summarize what the customer wants in one sentence. Check what they’ve already tried before asking for more information.
2. **Verify what applies.** Check the current knowledge base and any account or incident information you’re authorized to view. If the answer depends on a missing detail, ask a focused question. Follow the team’s identity-verification process before sharing account-specific information or making changes.
3. **Reply with the next step.** Lead with the answer or what you need from the customer. Give steps in the order they should try them. If you need more time, say what you’re checking and when they can expect an update you can commit to.
4. **Escalate when the case exceeds your authority.** Follow the team’s urgent escalation process for suspected account compromise, privacy concerns, or possible widespread outages. For actions that require approval, route the request to the authorized owner. Include the customer’s goal, impact, what you verified, what you tried, and the next update due. Confirm who owns the follow-up.
5. **Leave the ticket ready for the next person.** Record the outcome, any pending action, its owner, and the next update due. Use the team’s status and tagging rules. Close the ticket when it meets the team’s closure criteria.

**Practice case.** A customer says they requested a password reset twice and received neither email. They also ask you to change the account email. Acknowledge what they already tried, check the current recovery procedure, and follow the verification rules for email changes. If you lack permission to make the change, route it to the authorized owner and tell the customer when to expect an update.

## 3. Move to independent queue work

| Stage | What to do | Completion check |
|---|---|---|
| Shadow | Follow a teammate through a routine ticket and an escalation. | You can explain why they chose each next step and who owned the follow-up. |
| Practice | Draft a routine reply and prepare an escalation handoff using a training case or a supervisor-assigned ticket. | Your reviewer confirms that the reply is accurate, the handoff has enough context, and the ticket status matches the next action. |
| Take ownership | Work assigned tickets with review before sending. Ask your lead to review a completed case with you. | Your lead confirms which ticket types you can handle independently and which still require review or escalation. |

*This guide uses generic roles and workflows because team-specific tools, policies, and response targets weren’t provided. No vault note was saved because the exercise was completed without tools.*
