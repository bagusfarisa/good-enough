# Customer Support Onboarding Guide

Your first-week goal is to handle a routine support ticket from intake to follow-up with your trainer’s approval. Your trainer will show you which tickets you can handle on your own and who to contact when a case needs help.

## Before you take your first ticket

Ask your trainer or team lead to confirm the following during your first shift:

| What you need | What to confirm |
|---|---|
| Access | You can sign in to the support inbox, knowledge base, and internal help channel. You know where to report access problems. |
| Queue and coverage | You know which queue to watch, how to prioritize tickets, which response targets apply, and how to hand off work at the end of your shift. |
| Customer verification | You know the approved steps for verifying a customer before sharing account information or making account changes. |
| Escalation | You know where to route suspected security or privacy issues, outages, and requests outside your authority. You know who to contact if the usual owner is unavailable. |
| Practice | You have a trainer, a place to practice, and a way to get a draft reviewed before sending it. |

## Work a ticket from start to finish

1. **Read the full conversation.** Identify what the customer wants, what they’ve already tried, and any previous promises. Check for related tickets or a known incident before repeating troubleshooting.
2. **Check the approved guidance.** Find the relevant knowledge-base article or policy. If action requires verification or approval, follow that process before proceeding. For a suspected security or privacy issue, use the designated escalation route as soon as you recognize it.
3. **Reply with the next useful step.** Acknowledge the specific problem and give the answer or the next action in plain language. If you need more information, ask for the minimum needed through the approved channel. If you’ll follow up, give a time you can meet.
4. **Record the outcome.** Add the relevant actions, results, and commitments to the ticket. Set the status according to your queue’s rules. If another team takes over, include the customer’s goal, what you checked, why you’re escalating, and who owns the next customer update.

**Example.** A customer says they were charged twice and asks for a refund. Check the billing guidance and follow the approved verification steps before discussing account details. If a refund requires approval, route the case to the billing owner, record what you verified, and tell the customer when to expect an update. Keep the ticket in the appropriate pending state while the request is reviewed.

## Practice and get cleared for independent work

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| First shift | Review a resolved ticket and an escalated ticket with your trainer. Explain why each took a different path and who owned the final update. |
| Next shifts | Draft replies for routine cases. Have your trainer review the answer, any policy checks, the ticket status, and the follow-up before you send. |
| Before working independently | Complete one routine case under supervision and walk through one escalation scenario. Ask your trainer to confirm which queues and case types you’re cleared to handle. |

You’re ready for your assigned queue when you can explain the customer’s goal, show the guidance you used, and leave a ticket another teammate can pick up without asking what happened or what comes next.

*Team-specific tools, response targets, and escalation contacts were not provided. Confirm them with your trainer during the first shift. The no-tools exercise also prevented saving a copy to the shared vault.*
